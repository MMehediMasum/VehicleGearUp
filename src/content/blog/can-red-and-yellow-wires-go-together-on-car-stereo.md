## Car Stereo Wiring Basics

Understanding car stereo wiring basics is essential for safe and effective installation. Wires carry power and signals between your car and stereo system. Each wire color has a specific role to ensure the stereo works properly. Knowing these roles helps avoid mistakes and damage.

The wiring colors follow a common standard in most car stereos. This standard makes it easier to connect the stereo correctly, even without deep technical knowledge. Paying attention to wire colors simplifies the whole process.

### Common Wire Colors

* **Yellow:**Constant 12V power from the battery
* **Red:**Accessory or switched power
* **Black:**Ground connection
* **Blue:**Power antenna or amplifier turn-on
* **Speaker wires:**Usually pairs of colors with stripes

### Function Of Red Wire

The red wire provides switched power. It gets power only when the ignition is on. This wire powers the stereo when the car starts. It helps turn the stereo off when the car is off.

### Function Of Yellow Wire

The yellow wire connects directly to the battery. It supplies constant power to keep memory settings like radio presets and clock. This wire ensures settings are saved even if the car turns off.

These two wires serve different power functions that keep your stereo running smoothly. Mixing them up can lead to frustrating issues like losing your saved settings or even damaging your stereo.

### Power Source Differences

The yellow wire usually connects to a constant power source. This means it gets power directly from the battery at all times, even when your car is off. It keeps your stereo’s memory alive so your radio presets and clock settings don’t reset every time you turn off the ignition.

On the other hand, the red wire is tied to a switched power source. It only receives power when your car’s ignition is turned on. This wire powers the stereo’s main functions like playing music or using Bluetooth, so the system is active only when you want it to be.

### Ignition Vs. Constant Power

Think about what happens when you turn off your car. The red wire’s power cuts off, which should shut down the stereo. But the yellow wire keeps drawing power to maintain your stereo’s memory. This separation is crucial because if both wires were connected to the same source, you’d lose your saved settings every time you switched off the ignition.

Have you ever noticed your radio presets disappearing after turning off your car? That’s a clear sign the yellow wire isn’t connected properly to constant power. Checking these connections can save you from that annoying reset every time you start your drive.
