**Are you wondering if car dealerships give you license plates when you buy a car? It’s a question many buyers have but rarely get a clear answer to.**

Knowing what to expect can save you time and avoid surprises after your purchase. You’ll find straightforward answers and helpful tips about getting your license plates. Keep reading to make your car buying experience smoother and stress-free.



## License Plate Basics

Understanding license plates is key when you’re buying a car. They’re more than just tags on your vehicle—they’re your legal ID on the road. Knowing the basics helps you avoid surprises, especially about who provides them and how they work.

### Types Of License Plates

License plates come in different types, each serving a unique purpose. Standard plates are the most common and usually have a combination of letters and numbers.

Specialty plates, like those for veterans, universities, or causes, show personal interests or affiliations. There are also temporary plates that dealerships often provide when you first buy a car.

Have you ever wondered why some plates look different even within the same state? That’s because there are also personalized plates where you can choose the numbers or letters, making your car stand out.

### State Regulations On Plates

Each state has its own rules about license plates. Some states require you to get plates directly from the DMV, while others allow dealerships to provide them.

In some cases, you’ll get permanent plates right away, but other states issue temporary tags that you must replace soon after. These regulations can affect how quickly you hit the road legally.

Do you know what your state requires? Checking this before buying a car can save you time and prevent legal headaches.




## Role Of Car Dealerships

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=car-dealerships-play-a-key-role-in-helping-buyers-get-their-vehicles-on-the-road-they-often-manage-much-of-the-paperwork-and-legal-requirements-involved&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Car dealerships play a key role in helping buyers get their vehicles on the road. They often manage much of the paperwork and legal requirements involved. This includes handling license plates and registration. Understanding their role can help buyers know what to expect after purchasing a car.

### Temporary Plates From Dealers

Dealerships usually provide temporary plates for new cars. These plates allow you to drive legally while waiting for permanent plates. The temporary tags often last from 30 to 90 days. They display a registration number and expiration date. This gives the DMV time to process your permanent registration.

Temporary plates are easy to get from the dealer. They handle most of the paperwork to issue these plates. You do not need to visit the DMV immediately after buying the car.

### Permanent Plates Handling

Car dealerships often assist with permanent plate registration. They submit your documents to the DMV on your behalf. This process includes title transfer and fee payments. The DMV then mails your permanent plates to either you or the dealer.

Some states require you to pick up the plates yourself. Others send the plates directly to your address. The dealer will inform you how to receive your permanent plates. This service saves time and reduces hassle for new car owners.

  


## When Dealers Provide Plates

Car dealerships often handle license plates for you, especially when buying a new vehicle. Some provide temporary plates, while others may give permanent ones, depending on state rules. This service helps you drive legally right after the purchase.

When buying a car, many wonder about license plates. Do dealerships provide them? This can vary based on certain factors. Let's explore when dealers typically provide plates.

### New Car Purchases

Dealerships often provide plates for new cars. They handle registration and paperwork. This service eases the buying process. It allows you to drive legally right away. Some states require temporary tags first. The dealer arranges these too. After a few weeks, permanent plates arrive. This convenience appeals to many buyers.

### Used Car Purchases

Used car purchases differ slightly. Some dealerships offer plates, others don't. It depends on state laws and dealership policies. Dealers might provide temporary tags for used cars. These allow driving while waiting for permanent plates. Always ask your dealer about their policy. This ensures clarity and avoids surprises later.

## Situations Without Dealer Plates

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=not-all-car-purchases-come-with-dealer-plates-there-are-specific-situations-when-you-will-not-receive-these-plates-from-a-dealership&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Not all car purchases come with dealer plates. There are specific situations when you will not receive these plates from a dealership. Understanding these cases helps avoid confusion and legal troubles. Some common examples include private sales and out-of-state purchases. Each has unique rules about license plates and registration.

### Private Sales

Private sales happen between individuals without dealer involvement. In these cases, dealers do not issue license plates. Buyers must handle registration and plate acquisition themselves. Usually, the seller’s plates are removed before the sale. The new owner must visit the local motor vehicle office. There, they apply for new plates and title transfer. This process can take days or weeks depending on the state.

### Out-of-state Purchases

Buying a car from another state often means no dealer plates are provided. Out-of-state dealers may not have the authority to issue plates for your home state. Buyers must follow their state’s rules for registering the vehicle. Temporary tags might be available for transport. After arriving home, the buyer applies for permanent plates. This step ensures the vehicle is legal to drive locally.

## How To Get Plates If Not From Dealer

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=sometimes-car-dealerships-dont-provide-license-plates-right-away-leaving-you-to-figure-out-how-to-get-them-yourself-knowing-what-to-do-next-can-save-you-time-and-prevent-unnecessary-stress&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Sometimes, car dealerships don't provide license plates right away, leaving you to figure out how to get them yourself. Knowing what to do next can save you time and prevent unnecessary stress. Whether it's your first time or you want to avoid the dealer's extra fees, understanding alternative ways to get your plates is essential.

### Dmv Procedures

You can visit your local Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) office to register your vehicle and get your license plates. Make sure to bring all necessary documents like your vehicle title, proof of insurance, and ID. The process usually involves filling out a registration form and paying a fee.

DMVs often offer temporary plates if your permanent ones aren’t ready immediately. These allow you to legally drive while waiting for official plates. Some states may require an appointment, so check your DMV's website to avoid long waits.

### Online Plate Registration

Many states now offer online services to register your vehicle and order license plates. This option saves you a trip to the DMV and lets you complete the process from home. You'll need digital copies of your documents and a valid credit or debit card for payment.

After submitting your application online, plates are usually mailed to your address within a couple of weeks. Keep track of your confirmation number to check the status. If you want to avoid paperwork and lines, online registration is worth considering.




## Temporary Vs Permanent Plates

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=when-you-buy-a-car-you-often-get-either-temporary-or-permanent-license-plates-these-plates-let-you-drive-your-new-vehicle-legally-while-following-state-rules&w=424&h=324&c=7)

When you buy a car, you often get either temporary or permanent license plates. These plates let you drive your new vehicle legally while following state rules. Understanding the difference helps you know what to expect from the dealership and how long you can use each plate type.

### Duration And Validity

Temporary plates last for a short time, usually 30 to 90 days. They allow you to drive while waiting for permanent plates. Permanent plates stay on your car for years and are linked to your vehicle’s registration.

Temporary plates often come on paper or cardboard. Permanent plates are made of metal and more durable. The dealership usually gives you temporary plates immediately after purchase. Permanent plates arrive later by mail or are picked up at the DMV.

### Legal Requirements

States require temporary plates to show that your car is registered. You must display these plates clearly on the back of your vehicle. Driving without any plates can result in fines or tickets.

Permanent plates must be installed once they arrive. Some states need you to return the temporary plates to the DMV. Dealerships sometimes handle paperwork for you, but you remain responsible for following the law.

## Fees And Costs

Understanding the fees and costs related to license plates helps avoid surprises. Car dealerships often charge specific fees for handling plates. State fees also add to the total cost. Knowing these charges makes the buying process smoother.

### Dealer Fees For Plates

Dealers usually charge a fee for processing your license plates. This fee covers paperwork and administrative work. It varies by dealership and location but often ranges from $50 to $150. Some dealers include this fee in the total price of the car. Others list it separately. Always ask for a clear breakdown of dealer fees before buying.

### State Plate Fees

States charge fees to issue and register license plates. These fees differ widely across the country. They depend on factors like plate type, vehicle weight, and registration length. Typical fees can range from $20 to over $200. Some states require additional taxes or renewal fees. Check your state’s DMV website for exact amounts. Understanding these fees helps you budget properly for your new car.

## Common Myths About Dealer Plates

Dealer plates often confuse car buyers. Many believe myths about how these plates work. These myths can cause misunderstandings about what dealers provide. Clearing up these myths helps buyers know what to expect.

### Dealer Plates Are The Same As Regular License Plates

Dealer plates differ from regular plates. They are temporary and for dealer use only. Dealers use them for test drives or moving cars. They cannot be used like normal license plates.

### Dealerships Automatically Give You Plates With Purchase

Dealers do not always give plates immediately. Some states require buyers to get plates themselves. Dealers may issue temporary tags instead. These tags last only a short time.

### Dealer Plates Can Be Used On Any Vehicle

Dealer plates have limits. They are linked to the dealership's inventory. Buyers cannot use dealer plates on their personal cars. Using them wrongly can cause legal issues.

### Temporary Tags Are The Same As Dealer Plates

Temporary tags and dealer plates serve different purposes. Temporary tags are for new car buyers. Dealer plates are for dealer business use only. Each has specific rules and time limits.
