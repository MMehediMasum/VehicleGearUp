## How All-wheel Drive Works

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=all-wheel-drive-awd-helps-vehicles-handle-different-road-conditions-it-sends-power-to-all-four-wheels-instead-of-just-two&w=424&h=324&c=7)

All-wheel drive (AWD) helps vehicles handle different road conditions. It sends power to all four wheels instead of just two. This improves control and stability. Understanding how AWD works reveals why it’s useful for the Volvo XC90.

### Basic Awd Mechanism

AWD uses a system of differentials and sensors. These parts decide how much power each wheel needs. Power is shared between front and rear wheels. Some AWD systems can even adjust power to each wheel separately.

The system works automatically. It senses wheel slip or loss of traction. Then it sends more power to wheels with better grip. This process happens quickly to keep the vehicle steady.

### Benefits For Traction

AWD improves traction on slippery or uneven surfaces. It helps in rain, snow, mud, and gravel. The system reduces wheel spin by balancing power.

This makes driving safer and more confident. The Volvo XC90 AWD keeps you moving smoothly. It also provides better handling on curves and rough roads.

## Maintenance Of Awd System

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=maintaining-the-awd-system-in-your-volvo-xc90-is-crucial-to-ensure-it-performs-reliably-especially-in-challenging-road-conditions-the-system-depends-on-various-mechanical-and-electronic-components-working-together-smoothly&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Maintaining the AWD system in your Volvo XC90 is crucial to ensure it performs reliably, especially in challenging road conditions. The system depends on various mechanical and electronic components working together smoothly. Regular upkeep not only extends the life of your vehicle but also keeps you safe on the road.

### Routine Checks

Checking your AWD system regularly can prevent costly repairs. Pay attention to the transfer case fluid level and condition, as it lubricates key parts and helps prevent overheating. Volvo recommends inspecting this fluid every 30,000 to 50,000 miles, but checking it more often if you drive in rough conditions can save trouble.

Also, keep an eye on the tires—uneven wear can signal AWD problems. Make sure all four tires have similar tread depth and pressure. This keeps the system balanced and avoids unnecessary strain.

### Common Issues

Some common problems with the AWD system include worn-out clutches in the center differential, leaks in the transfer case, and sensor malfunctions. These issues often cause unusual noises, vibrations, or warning lights on your dashboard. Ignoring these signs can lead to more serious damage.

Have you noticed any delay in power delivery or difficulty turning sharply? These could be early symptoms of AWD troubles. Early diagnosis and fixing minor issues can save you from expensive repairs down the line.
