**If you’re working on your 350 Chevy engine, installing the distributor correctly is key to making sure your ride runs smoothly. You might think it’s a tough job, but with the right steps, you can do it yourself and save time and money.**

This guide will walk you through each part of the process in simple terms, so you won’t miss a thing. Keep reading, and by the end, you’ll feel confident that your Chevy’s ignition system is set up perfectly. Let’s get your engine firing like it’s meant to!



## Tools And Materials Needed

Getting your hands on the right tools and materials is crucial before installing a distributor on a 350 Chevy. Without them, you could run into unnecessary delays or even damage your engine components. Knowing exactly what you need saves time and helps you stay organized throughout the process.

Imagine starting the job only to realize you don’t have a key wrench or the correct gasket. That’s frustrating and avoidable. Let’s break down what you’ll need to get the job done efficiently and correctly.

### Essential Hand Tools

* **Socket Wrench Set:**Various sizes, especially 5/8-inch and 1/2-inch sockets, are necessary for removing bolts and securing the distributor.
* **Screwdrivers:**Both flathead and Phillips screwdrivers will help with clamps and electrical connections.
* **Timing Light:**This tool is vital for adjusting the distributor timing once installed.
* **Feeler Gauge:**To check and adjust the gap on the points if you’re working with a points-type distributor.
* **Distributor Wrench:**A specialized wrench can make loosening and tightening the hold-down bolt easier in tight spaces.

### Replacement Parts And Materials

* **Distributor Gasket:**Always use a new gasket to prevent oil leaks and ensure a proper seal.
* **Distributor Cap and Rotor:**If you’re replacing the entire distributor, these parts usually come with it, but check before you start.
* **Point Set and Condenser:**Needed for mechanical distributors; replacing these can improve ignition performance.
* **Dielectric Grease:**Applying this on electrical connectors can protect against corrosion and improve connectivity.
* **Engine Oil:**A small amount may be needed to lubricate the distributor shaft before installation.

### Safety Gear And Workspace Preparation

* **Gloves:**Protect your hands from sharp edges and hot engine parts.
* **Safety Glasses:**Prevent debris or fluid splashes from reaching your eyes.
* **Clean Workspace:**A tidy and well-lit area helps you keep track of small parts and tools.
* **Rags or Shop Towels:**Useful for wiping off oil or cleaning surfaces before installing the distributor.




## Preparing The Engine

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=ensure-the-engine-is-cool-and-the-battery-is-disconnected-before-starting-remove-old-parts-and-clean-the-mounting-area-for-a-smooth-installation&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Ensure the engine is cool and the battery is disconnected before starting. Remove old parts and clean the mounting area for a smooth installation. Check that the engine is at top dead center (TDC) to align the distributor correctly.

Preparing your engine is crucial for installing a distributor on a 350 Chevy. This process ensures a smooth installation and prevents any mishaps. Follow these steps to set the stage for a successful installation.

### Disconnecting The Battery

Begin by disconnecting the battery. This step is vital for safety. It prevents electrical shocks and accidental starts. Remove the negative cable using a wrench. Ensure it is completely detached. This will keep your work area safe.

### Locating The Old Distributor

Next, locate the old distributor. It is usually near the center of the engine. The distributor is a cylindrical part with several wires. Find it on top of the engine block. Familiarize yourself with its position. This will make removal easier later.

## Removing The Old Distributor

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=removing-the-old-distributor-from-your-350-chevy-is-a-crucial-step-that-sets-the-stage-for-a-smooth-installation-of-the-new-one-taking-your-time-here-can-save-you-from-alignment-headaches-later&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Removing the old distributor from your 350 Chevy is a crucial step that sets the stage for a smooth installation of the new one. Taking your time here can save you from alignment headaches later. Pay close attention to the rotor's position and wiring to ensure everything fits perfectly when you reinstall.

### Marking The Rotor Position

Before you touch the distributor, check the rotor's current position. Use a marker or a piece of tape to note where the rotor points on the distributor housing or the engine block. This small action helps maintain the engine’s timing and prevents guesswork when installing the new distributor.

Have you ever removed a part only to realize you forgot its original position? Avoid that frustration here by marking clearly. It’s a simple step that can save hours of troubleshooting later.

### Disconnecting Wiring And Components

Next, carefully disconnect the wiring harness connected to the distributor. Most of these connectors are clipped or snapped in place, so gently pry them off to avoid damage. Don’t forget to also remove any vacuum lines or other small components attached to the distributor body.

Keeping track of each wire and component you disconnect is essential. Consider taking a quick photo with your phone for reference. It’s easier than labeling every wire and will speed up your reassembly process.

### Removing Mounting Bolts

Once the wiring is free, locate and remove the mounting bolts that hold the distributor in place. These bolts can be snug, so use the correct size socket and apply steady pressure to avoid stripping. Keep the bolts safe; you’ll need them to secure the new distributor.

After removing the bolts, gently lift the distributor straight out. You might notice some resistance due to the gears meshing, so wiggle it slightly without forcing. If it feels stuck, double-check for any missed connections or bolts.




## Installing The New Distributor

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=installing-the-new-distributor-on-your-350-chevy-is-a-task-that-demands-attention-to-detail-getting-it-right-affects-your-engines-timing-and-overall-performance&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Installing the new distributor on your 350 Chevy is a task that demands attention to detail. Getting it right affects your engine's timing and overall performance. Take your time, and focus on precise placement to ensure your engine runs smoothly.

### Aligning The Rotor And Distributor

Before placing the distributor, make sure the rotor points to the number one cylinder position on the cap. This alignment is crucial because it determines when the spark hits the right cylinder. If the rotor is off by even a little, the engine might misfire or struggle to start.

Turn the engine to top dead center (TDC) on the compression stroke for cylinder one. This gives you a reliable reference point. Once at TDC, position the rotor accordingly to match this setting.

### Seating The Distributor Properly

Lower the distributor slowly into the engine block. You might feel the distributor gear engage with the camshaft gear—don’t force it. If you meet resistance, slightly rotate the rotor back and forth to help the gears mesh smoothly.

The distributor should sit flush and snug without wobbling. If it’s not seated well, you risk timing issues and potential engine damage. Take a moment to double-check the fit before moving on.

### Securing With Bolts

Once the distributor is seated correctly, secure it with the hold-down bolt. Tighten the bolt just enough to keep the distributor from moving but loose enough to allow fine adjustments. This balance is key for tuning your timing later.

After securing, try gently rotating the distributor body to confirm it holds firm but can be adjusted with some effort. This step often separates a quick fix from a perfect tune.

## Setting The Timing

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=setting-the-timing-on-a-350-chevy-is-essential-after-installing-the-distributor-proper-timing-ensures-the-engine-runs-smoothly-and-efficiently&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Setting the timing on a 350 Chevy is essential after installing the distributor. Proper timing ensures the engine runs smoothly and efficiently. It controls when the spark plugs fire in relation to the piston position. Incorrect timing can cause poor performance or engine damage.

### Reconnecting Wiring

Start by reconnecting all distributor wires carefully. Match each wire to its correct terminal. Loose or wrong connections can cause engine misfires. Check the coil wire and the spark plug wires. Make sure everything is tight and secure. This step is crucial before adjusting the timing.

### Using A Timing Light

A timing light helps check the ignition timing accurately. Connect the light’s inductive pickup to the number one spark plug wire. Attach the power clamps to the battery terminals. Start the engine and let it idle. Aim the timing light at the timing marks on the harmonic balancer. The light will flash, showing the timing position clearly.

### Adjusting The Distributor

Loosen the distributor hold-down clamp slightly. Turn the distributor slowly while watching the timing marks. Rotate it until the marks line up with the specified timing degree. Tighten the clamp once the timing is correct. Recheck with the timing light to confirm. Proper adjustment improves engine power and fuel economy.

## Final Checks And Testing

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=after-installing-the-distributor-on-your-350-chevy-its-crucial-to-perform-final-checks-and-testing-to-ensure-everything-runs-smoothly-skipping-these-steps-can-lead-to-engine-misfires-poor-performance-or-worse-damage-to-your-engine&w=424&h=324&c=7)

After installing the distributor on your 350 Chevy, it’s crucial to perform final checks and testing to ensure everything runs smoothly. Skipping these steps can lead to engine misfires, poor performance, or worse, damage to your engine. Taking the time to verify each connection and test the system will give you confidence that your hard work paid off.

### Reconnecting The Battery

Before you reconnect the battery, double-check that all tools and loose parts are clear from the engine bay. This helps prevent accidental shorts or damage when power is restored.

When you connect the battery terminals, ensure the positive terminal goes on first, followed by the negative. This simple order reduces the risk of sparks and electrical issues.

### Starting The Engine

Turn the key and listen carefully as the engine cranks. If it doesn't start immediately, don't panic. Sometimes it takes a few tries for the fuel system to prime and the ignition timing to settle.

If the engine stalls or runs rough, consider checking the distributor’s position and spark plug wires. Small adjustments can make a big difference in ignition timing and engine behavior.

### Verifying Performance

Once the engine is running, observe how it idles. A smooth, steady idle usually means the distributor is correctly installed. If you notice hesitation or backfiring, it may indicate timing issues.

Take your car for a short test drive. Pay attention to acceleration and engine response. Does the engine pull smoothly, or does it stumble under load? These clues help you fine-tune the distributor settings.

Ask yourself: Are you hearing any unusual noises or smells? These signals often point to ignition or fuel delivery problems. Fixing them early prevents bigger headaches down the road.

  



## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Align The Distributor On A 350 Chevy?

To align the distributor, rotate the engine to top dead center (TDC) on cylinder one. Match the rotor with the number one spark plug wire position. This ensures proper timing and efficient engine performance.

### What Tools Are Needed To Install A 350 Chevy Distributor?

You need a wrench set, timing light, distributor gasket, and a screwdriver. These tools help remove the old distributor, install the new one, and set the timing accurately.

### How To Set The Ignition Timing After Installation?

Use a timing light to adjust the distributor until the timing marks align. This process ensures the engine runs smoothly and prevents misfires or poor fuel economy.

### Can I Install A Distributor Without Removing The Engine?

Yes, the distributor can be installed without engine removal. Just ensure the engine is at TDC for cylinder one and carefully insert the distributor shaft.
