In this guide, you’ll learn simple, clear steps to get the job done right—even if you’ve never done it before. Keep reading, and you’ll gain the confidence to tune your engine like a pro.




## Tools Needed

Adjusting the valves on a 350 Chevy engine needs the right tools. Having these tools ready makes the task easier and safer. Each tool plays an important role in the process. Some tools help you measure, while others help you turn or hold parts.

Prepare all tools before starting. This saves time and avoids frustration during the job. Here is a list of essential tools for adjusting the valves on a 350 Chevy.

### Feeler Gauges

Feeler gauges measure the gap between the valve and rocker arm. Accurate measurement ensures proper valve clearance. Use a set with different thickness blades for precise adjustment.

### Socket Set And Ratchet

A socket set helps remove bolts and covers on the valve train. A ratchet provides the leverage needed to loosen or tighten fasteners quickly. Choose sizes that fit your engine bolts perfectly.

### Screwdriver

A flathead screwdriver adjusts the valve lash on the rocker arm. It helps turn the adjustment screw smoothly. Make sure the screwdriver fits well to avoid slipping.

### Torque Wrench

A torque wrench tightens bolts to the correct specification. Over-tightening or under-tightening can cause engine damage. Use this tool to ensure bolts are secure but not too tight.

### Wrench Or Spanner

A wrench holds the rocker arm nut while adjusting the valve lash. This prevents the nut from turning while you turn the adjustment screw. Select the correct size for your engine.

## Preparing The Engine

Preparing the engine is a crucial first step before adjusting the valves on a 350 Chevy. A clean and accessible workspace helps avoid damage and ensures accurate adjustments. Taking time to prepare will save effort and prevent mistakes.

### Remove Valve Covers

Start by disconnecting the battery to avoid accidental sparks. Remove any hoses or wires connected to the valve covers. Use a wrench to loosen the bolts holding the valve covers in place. Gently lift the covers off, exposing the rocker arms and valves underneath. Place the valve covers on a clean surface to prevent dirt from entering.

### Clean The Area

Remove any dirt, grease, or debris from the exposed engine parts. Use a clean rag or a soft brush for this task. Avoid pushing dirt into the valve train. A clean area helps identify worn parts and prevents contamination. Proper cleaning also makes valve adjustment easier and more precise.

## Locate Top Dead Center

Finding Top Dead Center (TDC) is key for adjusting the valves on a 350 Chevy engine. Rotate the crankshaft until the number one piston reaches its highest point. This ensures accurate valve clearance settings and proper engine timing.

Adjusting the valves on a 350 Chevy engine is a task that demands precision and patience. One of the crucial steps in this process is locating the Top Dead Center (TDC) of the engine. Understanding this concept is vital because it ensures that the valves are adjusted when the piston is at the right point in its cycle. Missing this step can result in improper valve clearance, which affects engine performance.

### Identify Cylinder One

To start, you need to identify cylinder one. On a 350 Chevy, this is typically the cylinder at the front of the engine on the driver's side. Knowing the layout of your engine is crucial, as it sets the stage for the entire valve adjustment process. Have you ever noticed how a simple mix-up here can throw off the entire operation? That's why confirming the position of cylinder one is so important.

### Rotate The Crankshaft

Next, rotate the crankshaft to position the piston at TDC. This is done by turning the crankshaft with a socket wrench. Keep an eye on the timing marks on the harmonic balancer. When these marks align, you have reached TDC for cylinder one. Don't rush this step. Take your time to ensure the marks align perfectly. Doesn't it feel rewarding when you see those marks line up just right? Your patience here lays the groundwork for a smooth adjustment process. ## Check Valve Clearance

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=checking-valve-clearance-on-a-350-chevy-engine-is-crucial-to-ensure-your-engine-runs-smoothly-and-efficiently-too-tight-or-too-loose-clearances-can-cause-poor-engine-performance-or-even-damage-over-time&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Checking valve clearance on a 350 Chevy engine is crucial to ensure your engine runs smoothly and efficiently. Too tight or too loose clearances can cause poor engine performance or even damage over time. Taking the time to measure and adjust these clearances correctly can save you from costly repairs down the line.

### Use Feeler Gauge

A feeler gauge is the tool you need to measure the gap between the valve stem and the rocker arm. It comes in thin metal strips of varying thicknesses. Slide the feeler gauge between the valve and rocker arm to find the clearance.

You want a slight drag on the gauge, not too tight or too loose. If the gauge slides in too easily or doesn’t fit at all, the clearance needs adjusting. It’s a simple tool but provides precise results when used carefully.

### Compare To Specs

After measuring, compare the valve clearance to the factory specifications for your 350 Chevy. These specs are usually found in the service manual or online resources. Each valve has a specific clearance range you must stay within.

For example, intake valves might require 0.015 inches and exhaust valves 0.020 inches, but always verify for your exact model year. If your measurements fall outside these numbers, it’s time to make adjustments to avoid engine issues.

## Adjusting The Valves

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=adjusting-the-valves-on-your-350-chevy-is-a-key-maintenance-task-that-ensures-your-engine-runs-smoothly-and-efficiently-proper-valve-clearance-prevents-engine-noise-improves-performance-and-extends-the-life-of-your-components&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Adjusting the valves on your 350 Chevy is a key maintenance task that ensures your engine runs smoothly and efficiently. Proper valve clearance prevents engine noise, improves performance, and extends the life of your components. Let’s break down the process into manageable steps to help you get it right.

### Loosen Lock Nut

Start by loosening the lock nut on the valve adjuster. Use a wrench that fits snugly to avoid rounding off the nut. Loosening this nut allows you to turn the adjuster screw freely when setting the clearance.

It’s a good idea to mark the position of the lock nut before loosening it. This helps you keep track of how much you’ve moved it and ensures you can tighten it back without disturbing your adjustment.

### Set Proper Clearance

Next, set the proper clearance between the rocker arm and the valve stem. Use a feeler gauge that matches the specification for your 350 Chevy—typically around 0.015 inches for intake valves and 0.020 inches for exhaust valves.

Slide the correct thickness gauge between the rocker arm and valve stem, then turn the adjuster screw until you feel a slight drag on the gauge. This means you have the right clearance—not too tight and not too loose.

### Tighten Lock Nut

After setting the clearance, hold the adjuster screw steady with a screwdriver or Allen wrench. Then, tighten the lock nut carefully without changing the position of the adjuster.

Check the clearance one more time after tightening to confirm nothing shifted. This double-check can save you from redoing the whole process, saving time and frustration.

## Repeat For All Valves

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=adjusting-the-valves-on-a-350-chevy-requires-precision-for-each-valve-the-process-does-not-end-after-adjusting-one-valve&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Adjusting the valves on a 350 Chevy requires precision for each valve. The process does not end after adjusting one valve. It is important to repeat the same careful steps for every valve in the engine. This ensures smooth engine performance and prevents damage.

Each valve must be checked and adjusted individually. The correct gap must be set according to the manufacturer’s specifications. Skipping a valve or rushing through can cause engine problems later.

### Check Each Valve Position

Rotate the engine to bring each valve into the proper position. Only adjust valves that are fully closed. This is when the cam lobe is pointing away from the valve.

### Use The Feeler Gauge Correctly

Slide the feeler gauge between the valve stem and rocker arm. The gauge should fit snugly but move with slight resistance. This confirms the gap is correct.

### Adjust The Valve Clearance

Turn the adjusting nut to increase or decrease the gap. Tighten or loosen until the feeler gauge fits properly. Avoid over-tightening, which can cause damage.

### Double-check All Valves

After adjusting, recheck each valve with the feeler gauge. Confirm all gaps match the recommended measurements. This step ensures accuracy and engine reliability.

  


## Reassemble And Test

After adjusting the valves on your 350 Chevy, reassembling and testing the engine is crucial. This step ensures everything fits well and works smoothly. Proper reassembly prevents leaks and keeps the engine running quiet and efficient. Testing confirms the valves are set correctly and the engine performs at its best.

### Replace Valve Covers

Place the valve covers back onto the cylinder heads carefully. Make sure the gasket sits correctly to avoid oil leaks. Tighten the bolts evenly in a crisscross pattern. Do not overtighten, as this can damage the gasket or cover. Check that all bolts are snug to keep the cover secure.

### Start Engine And Listen

Start the engine and let it run at idle speed. Listen closely for unusual noises like ticking or knocking. A smooth, quiet sound means the valves are adjusted properly. If you hear any noise, turn off the engine and recheck your adjustments. Repeat until the engine runs quietly and smoothly.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Often Should I Adjust The Valves On A 350 Chevy?

Valves on a 350 Chevy should be adjusted every 12,000 to 15,000 miles. Regular adjustments ensure optimal engine performance and prevent damage.

### What Tools Are Needed To Adjust 350 Chevy Valves?

You need a feeler gauge, socket set, wrench, and screwdriver. These tools help measure and adjust the valve clearance accurately.

### How Do I Know If My 350 Chevy Valves Need Adjustment?

Symptoms include rough idle, engine noise, and reduced power. If you notice these, checking and adjusting the valves can help.

### Can I Adjust Valves On A 350 Chevy Without Removing The Intake?

No, you must remove the intake manifold for proper valve access. This ensures accurate clearance measurement and adjustment.
