### Operating The Air Compressor

First, park your vehicle near the air compressor station. Locate the hose with the air nozzle, which is designed for easy handling. Remove the cap from your tire valve and firmly press the nozzle onto the valve stem.

Press the trigger or button on the nozzle to start the airflow. Keep an eye on your tire pressure gauge if you have one, or use the built-in gauge on the compressor. Release the trigger when you reach the recommended pressure, then replace your tire valve cap securely.

It’s important to avoid over-inflating. Have you ever felt your car ride was too stiff after pumping too much air? Proper inflation improves safety and fuel efficiency, so take your time with the process.

## Tips For Checking And Maintaining Tire Pressure

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=maintaining-the-right-tire-pressure-is-essential-for-safe-driving-and-better-fuel-efficiency-many-drivers-underestimate-how-often-tire-pressure-should-be-checked-and-what-the-correct-levels-are&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Maintaining the right tire pressure is essential for safe driving and better fuel efficiency. Many drivers underestimate how often tire pressure should be checked and what the correct levels are. Let’s dive into practical tips that will help you keep your tires in top shape and avoid common pitfalls.

### When To Check Tire Air

You should check your tire air at least once a month and before long trips. Cold tires give the most accurate reading, so measure pressure in the morning or after the car has been parked for a few hours.

Have you noticed your car pulling to one side or feeling less stable? That’s often a sign of uneven or low tire pressure. Checking more often during seasonal changes is smart since temperature shifts affect tire air.

### Recommended Tire Pressure Levels

Check the vehicle’s manual or the sticker inside the driver’s door for the exact recommended pressure. Typically, passenger cars require between 30 to 35 PSI, but this varies widely depending on the make and model.

| Vehicle Type | Recommended Tire Pressure (PSI) |
| --- | --- |
| Compact Cars | 30 - 35 |
| SUVs and Light Trucks | 35 - 40 |
| Heavy-Duty Trucks | 50+ |

Overinflating can cause uneven wear and a harsh ride, while underinflating lowers fuel efficiency and increases tire wear. Are you sure your tires are at the right pressure right now? Grab a reliable gauge and check—you might be surprised.
