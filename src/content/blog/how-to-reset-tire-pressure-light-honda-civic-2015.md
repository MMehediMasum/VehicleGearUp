## Tire Pressure Light Basics

The tire pressure light is an important warning on your 2015 Honda Civic dashboard. It helps keep you safe by showing when your tires need attention. Understanding this light can save you time and prevent damage to your car. This section explains what the tire pressure light means and why it turns on.

### What The Light Means

The tire pressure light signals low air pressure in one or more tires. Proper tire pressure is crucial for safe driving. Low pressure can cause poor handling and increase tire wear. The light is part of the Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS). It alerts you to check and fix tire pressure as soon as possible.

### Why It Turns On

The light turns on for several reasons:

* One or more tires have low air pressure.
* A tire has a slow leak or puncture.
* There is a sudden change in temperature affecting tire pressure.
* The TPMS sensor is faulty or needs replacement.

Ignoring the light can lead to unsafe driving conditions. Always check your tires when the light appears.




## Check Tire Pressure

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=checking-the-tire-pressure-is-the-first-step-to-reset-the-tire-pressure-light-on-your-2015-honda-civic-proper-tire-pressure-ensures-safe-driving-and-improves-fuel-efficiency&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Checking the tire pressure is the first step to reset the tire pressure light on your 2015 Honda Civic. Proper tire pressure ensures safe driving and improves fuel efficiency. Low or high pressure can trigger the warning light. Checking the pressure helps identify if adjustment is needed.

### Locate Recommended Psi

Find the correct tire pressure for your Honda Civic in the driver’s side door jamb. It is listed on a sticker showing the recommended PSI for front and rear tires. Use this number as your target when checking and adjusting the tire pressure.

### Use A Tire Pressure Gauge

Use a reliable tire pressure gauge to measure the air pressure in each tire. Remove the valve cap, press the gauge onto the valve stem, and read the number. Check all four tires and record their pressure levels for comparison.

### Adjust Tire Pressure

Add air if the tire pressure is below the recommended PSI. Use an air compressor at a gas station or a portable pump. Release air if the pressure is too high by pressing the valve stem. Recheck pressure to ensure it matches the recommended level.

## Resetting The Light Manually

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=resetting-the-tire-pressure-light-on-a-2015-honda-civic-can-be-done-manually-with-simple-steps-this-process-helps-clear-the-warning-after-adjusting-tire-pressures-or-fixing-leaks&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Resetting the tire pressure light on a 2015 Honda Civic can be done manually with simple steps. This process helps clear the warning after adjusting tire pressures or fixing leaks. Following these steps ensures the system properly detects the correct tire pressure.

### Turn Ignition On

Start by turning the ignition switch to the ON position. Do not start the engine. This action powers the vehicle’s electronic systems, including the tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS).

### Locate Tpms Button

Find the TPMS reset button inside the vehicle. It is usually located below the steering wheel or near the dashboard. Check near the hood release or inside the glove box if it is not immediately visible.

### Press And Hold The Button

Press and hold the TPMS button for about 3 seconds. The tire pressure light will blink several times, indicating the reset process has started. Release the button and wait for the light to turn off. This confirms the system has reset successfully.

Start by turning on the ignition without starting the engine. Use the touchscreen on the infotainment system. Look for the “Settings” icon on the main menu. Tap it to open vehicle-related options. This area controls many car features, including tire pressure monitoring.

### Navigate To Tpms Reset

Inside the settings menu, find “Vehicle” or “Car” settings. Scroll down to locate the “TPMS” or “Tire Pressure Monitoring System” option. Select this to enter the reset menu. This section lets you manage tire pressure alerts and calibration.

### Confirm The Reset

After selecting the TPMS reset, the system asks for confirmation. Confirm the reset by tapping “Yes” or “OK.” The infotainment system then clears the tire pressure light. Turn off the ignition and start the car to check the light. If the tire pressures are correct, the warning light should be off.

## Troubleshooting Persistent Light

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=sometimes-the-tire-pressure-light-on-your-2015-honda-civic-stays-on-even-after-youve-reset-it-this-persistent-light-can-be-frustrating-signaling-that-something-might-still-be-wrong&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Sometimes, the tire pressure light on your 2015 Honda Civic stays on even after you’ve reset it. This persistent light can be frustrating, signaling that something might still be wrong. Before you panic, it’s important to troubleshoot carefully to understand the root cause and avoid unnecessary repairs or replacements.

### Check For Tire Damage

Start by examining each tire closely. Look for visible signs of damage like cuts, punctures, or embedded objects such as nails or glass shards.

Even a slow leak can keep the tire pressure low enough to trigger the warning light. You might not always hear a hissing sound, so check the tire pressure with a reliable gauge.

Have you ever driven with a nail lodged in your tire, thinking it was fine? That’s a common mistake that keeps the pressure low and the light on.

### Inspect Tpms Sensors

Your Civic’s Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) sensors could be the culprit if the light remains on. These sensors can fail due to battery depletion or damage from rough roads.

Try resetting the system again after checking the sensor status. If you have access to a TPMS tool, it can help identify which sensor is malfunctioning.

Remember, replacing a sensor battery isn’t always possible—you might need a full sensor replacement if it’s dead.

### When To Visit A Mechanic

If you’ve checked tires and sensors but the light won’t reset, it’s time to seek professional help. Mechanics have specialized equipment to diagnose TPMS issues accurately.

Ignoring a persistent tire pressure light could lead to uneven tire wear or even unsafe driving conditions. A quick visit can save you money and keep your car running smoothly.

Don’t wait for a flat tire or worse—ask your mechanic to inspect the entire tire pressure system to find hidden problems.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Reset The Tire Pressure Light On A 2015 Honda Civic?

To reset the tire pressure light, turn the ignition on without starting the engine. Press and hold the TPMS button until the light blinks twice. Release the button, then start the car. The light should turn off after a few minutes of driving.

### What Causes The Tire Pressure Light To Stay On?

The light stays on due to low tire pressure, a faulty sensor, or temperature changes. Check and inflate tires to the recommended PSI first. If the light remains, a sensor reset or replacement may be needed.

### Can I Reset The Tire Pressure Light Without Tools?

Yes, you can reset it manually using the TPMS button inside your Honda Civic. No special tools are required. Simply follow the reset procedure found in the owner’s manual or use the button near the steering wheel.

### How Often Should I Check Tire Pressure On A Honda Civic?

Check tire pressure at least once a month and before long trips. Proper tire pressure ensures safety, fuel efficiency, and prolongs tire life. Use a reliable gauge and inflate tires to the manufacturer’s recommended PSI.
