## Ford Explorer Drivetrain Options

The Ford Explorer offers a variety of drivetrain options designed to meet different driving needs and preferences. Understanding these options can help you decide which model suits your lifestyle best. Whether you prioritize fuel efficiency, traction, or off-road capability, the Explorer lineup has a drivetrain that fits.

### Rear Wheel Drive Explained

Rear Wheel Drive (RWD) means the engine powers the rear wheels only. This setup is common in trucks and SUVs like the Ford Explorer because it provides better towing and hauling capabilities.

RWD often feels more balanced and delivers a sporty driving experience on dry roads. However, it may struggle on slippery surfaces such as ice or wet pavement, so consider your typical driving conditions before choosing this option.

### All Wheel Drive Variants

All Wheel Drive (AWD) in the Ford Explorer sends power to all four wheels automatically, depending on road conditions. This improves traction on wet or snowy roads without any input from you.

AWD is perfect if you live in an area with unpredictable weather or want added confidence during your daily commute. It’s not as rugged as Four Wheel Drive but offers a good balance between performance and convenience.

### Four Wheel Drive Models

Four Wheel Drive (4WD) systems provide power to all four wheels simultaneously and are designed for serious off-road use. The Ford Explorer’s 4WD models include features like low-range gearing for tough terrains.

If you enjoy weekend adventures on rough trails or need a vehicle that can handle deep snow and mud, 4WD is a practical choice. It’s worth noting that 4WD usually consumes more fuel and requires more maintenance than RWD or AWD.

## Performance Impact Of Rear Wheel Drive

The performance impact of rear wheel drive (RWD) in the Ford Explorer shapes how the vehicle handles different driving conditions. RWD affects traction, fuel use, and the vehicle's ability to tow and carry weight. Understanding these effects helps drivers know what to expect on the road.

### Handling And Traction

Rear wheel drive offers balanced handling by sending power to the rear wheels. This setup improves steering control during turns and allows for smoother acceleration. On dry roads, RWD provides strong traction. However, on slippery surfaces like ice or wet pavement, traction can be less reliable than all-wheel or front-wheel drive systems.

### Fuel Efficiency

Vehicles with rear wheel drive often have a simpler drivetrain, which can lead to better fuel efficiency. The Ford Explorer’s RWD system reduces the energy lost in power transfer. This can result in slightly improved gas mileage compared to four-wheel drive models. Efficient fuel use means fewer trips to the pump and lower running costs.

### Towing And Payload

Rear wheel drive supports higher towing and payload capacities. The power delivery to the rear wheels helps push heavy loads without strain. This makes the Ford Explorer with RWD a good choice for hauling trailers or carrying heavy cargo. The design also helps maintain stability while towing, improving safety and control.




## Comparing Rear Wheel Drive With Other Systems

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=understanding-the-differences-between-rear-wheel-drive-and-other-drivetrain-systems-helps-you-make-a-smarter-choice-for-your-ford-explorer-or-any-vehicle-each-system-offers-unique-benefits-and-potential-drawbacks-depending-on-your-driving-needs-and-conditions&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Understanding the differences between rear wheel drive and other drivetrain systems helps you make a smarter choice for your Ford Explorer or any vehicle. Each system offers unique benefits and potential drawbacks depending on your driving needs and conditions. Let's break down how rear wheel drive stacks up against all wheel drive and four wheel drive.

### Rear Wheel Vs All Wheel Drive

Rear wheel drive (RWD) sends power to the back wheels only, giving a sportier, more balanced feel on dry roads. It often delivers better handling and acceleration, especially in performance settings.

All wheel drive (AWD) powers all four wheels simultaneously, which improves traction on slippery or uneven surfaces. This is great if you frequently encounter rain, snow, or light off-road conditions.

Consider how often you drive in tough weather. If you mostly drive on highways or city streets, RWD might be enough. But if you want extra security on wet or icy roads, AWD could be worth the extra cost and complexity.

### Rear Wheel Vs Four Wheel Drive

Four wheel drive (4WD) is designed for serious off-road use and challenging terrain. It can be manually switched on or off, allowing you to send power to all wheels when needed, then back to two wheels on easy roads.

Rear wheel drive is simpler and lighter, which usually means better fuel efficiency and less maintenance. However, it lacks the tough grip and control 4WD offers on rough trails or deep snow.

Think about your lifestyle. Do you go off-roading or face tough winter conditions often? If yes, 4WD might be the better choice. But for everyday driving with occasional bad weather, RWD can still perform well and save you money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is The Ford Explorer Rear Wheel Drive Or All-wheel Drive?

The Ford Explorer primarily features rear-wheel drive as standard. However, many models offer an all-wheel drive option for better traction and handling in various conditions. Buyers can choose based on their driving needs and preferences.

### Can You Upgrade A Ford Explorer To Rear Wheel Drive?

Most Ford Explorer models come with rear-wheel drive or all-wheel drive options from the factory. Upgrading between these drivetrains is complex and costly. It’s generally not practical to convert an all-wheel drive Explorer to rear-wheel drive.

### How Does Rear Wheel Drive Affect Ford Explorer Performance?

Rear-wheel drive in the Ford Explorer provides better balance and handling, especially during acceleration. It offers improved towing capacity and driving dynamics compared to front-wheel drive. However, it may perform less effectively in slippery conditions without all-wheel drive.

### Which Ford Explorer Models Have Rear Wheel Drive?

The rear-wheel drive configuration is standard in most base and mid-level Ford Explorer trims. Higher trims and off-road packages typically come with all-wheel drive. Check the specific model year and trim for exact drivetrain details.
