## Symptoms Of Full Accessory Power Active

The Full Accessory Power Active mode in a Ford Explorer can cause various issues. Recognizing the symptoms early helps prevent further damage. This mode affects how electrical components behave, leading to noticeable signs. Identifying these symptoms guides proper troubleshooting and repair.

### Common Warning Signs

* The engine does not start but accessories stay on.
* Interior lights, radio, and power windows work without ignition.
* Battery drains quickly even after the vehicle is off.
* Dashboard warning lights flicker or remain illuminated.
* Unusual sounds from the ignition switch or electrical system.

### Impact On Vehicle Performance

The Full Accessory Power Active mode can lower battery life. It may cause slow or no response when turning the ignition key. Electrical components may behave erratically or fail to work. The engine may stall or fail to start due to power issues. Overall vehicle reliability and safety decrease if the problem persists.




## Causes Of Full Accessory Power Active

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=the-full-accessory-power-active-mode-in-a-ford-explorer-can-cause-frustration-this-mode-allows-some-electrical-components-to-run-even-when-the-engine-is-off&w=424&h=324&c=7)

The Full Accessory Power Active mode in a Ford Explorer can cause frustration. This mode allows some electrical components to run even when the engine is off. Understanding the causes helps to find the right fix. Several factors can trigger this issue. Most relate to electrical or ignition system problems. Identifying the root cause saves time and money on repairs.

### Electrical System Issues

Faulty wiring or short circuits often cause Full Accessory Power Active. Damaged wires may send wrong signals to the car’s computer. A blown fuse or relay can also create power flow problems. These electrical faults keep the accessories powered unnecessarily. Regular inspection of wiring and fuses can prevent this issue.

### Battery And Alternator Problems

A weak or dying battery can confuse the power management system. The alternator, which charges the battery, might fail to keep it full. Low battery voltage may cause the vehicle to stay in accessory mode. Testing the battery and alternator health helps rule out these causes quickly.

### Faulty Ignition Switch

The ignition switch controls power to the vehicle’s electrical system. A worn or broken switch can send incorrect signals. This fault can make the Explorer think the key is in the accessory position. Replacing the ignition switch often resolves the problem.

  


## Basic Troubleshooting Steps

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=experiencing-the-full-accessory-power-active-warning-on-your-ford-explorer-can-be-concerning-however-before-rushing-to-the-mechanic-there-are-some-basic-troubleshooting-steps-you-can-take&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Experiencing the "Full Accessory Power Active" warning on your Ford Explorer can be concerning. However, before rushing to the mechanic, there are some basic troubleshooting steps you can take. Addressing these issues early can save time and money, and sometimes even a simple fix can resolve the problem. Here’s a breakdown of what you can do to troubleshoot this issue effectively.

### Checking Battery Health

Your vehicle's battery is the heart of its electrical system. Ensuring it’s in good health is crucial. Start by checking the battery terminals for corrosion. A clean terminal ensures a good connection. Also, consider using a multimeter to measure the voltage. A healthy battery should read around 12.6 volts when the engine is off. If the reading is significantly lower, it might be time for a replacement.

### Inspecting Fuses And Relays

Fuses and relays act as the gatekeepers of your car's electrical components. Open your vehicle’s fuse box and inspect for any blown fuses. These are typically easy to spot as they’ll have a broken wire inside. Make sure to replace any damaged ones with a fuse of the same amperage. Relays can be a bit trickier, but swapping a suspected faulty relay with a known good one can help diagnose the issue.

### Resetting The Vehicle’s Computer

Sometimes, a simple reset can clear glitches causing the warning message. Disconnect the negative battery cable for about 15 minutes to reset the vehicle's computer. Reconnect it, ensuring the connection is tight and free from corrosion. This process can often clear minor issues and is a straightforward step you can take at home. Tackling these basic steps can often resolve the "Full Accessory Power Active" warning. However, if the issue persists, consulting a professional mechanic is advisable. Remember, preventive maintenance and timely checks can prevent such issues from cropping up unexpectedly.

### Using An Obd-ii Scanner

An OBD-II scanner reads error codes from the vehicle’s computer system. These codes reveal faults related to the accessory power system. Connect the scanner to the port under the dashboard. Turn the ignition to the ON position without starting the engine. The scanner displays codes that guide troubleshooting. Clear codes after repairs to check if the problem returns.

### Testing The Ignition Switch

The ignition switch controls power to many vehicle systems. Faulty switches can cause the Full Accessory Power Active message. Use a multimeter to check for voltage at the switch terminals. Turn the key to different positions and observe the readings. Look for inconsistent voltage or no power at certain points. Replace the ignition switch if tests show failure.

### Evaluating Wiring And Connections

Damaged wiring and loose connections often cause electrical problems. Inspect wiring harnesses near the ignition and fuse box carefully. Look for frayed wires, corrosion, or loose connectors. Use a wiring diagram to trace circuits related to accessory power. Repair or replace damaged wires and secure all connections tightly. This ensures proper current flow and system stability.

## Quick Fixes To Try

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=when-the-full-accessory-power-active-light-stays-on-in-your-ford-explorer-quick-fixes-can-save-time-and-effort-these-simple-steps-can-restore-normal-function-without-costly-repairs&w=424&h=324&c=7)

When the Full Accessory Power Active light stays on in your Ford Explorer, quick fixes can save time and effort. These simple steps can restore normal function without costly repairs. Start with easy checks before seeking professional help.

### Battery Reconnection

Sometimes, the battery connection causes the power issue. Turn off the engine and open the hood. Locate the battery terminals. Remove the negative terminal first, then the positive. Clean any dirt or corrosion on the terminals with a wire brush. Reconnect the positive terminal first, then the negative. Tighten the clamps to ensure a solid connection. Start the car and check if the light goes off.

### Fuse Replacement

A blown fuse can trigger the Full Accessory Power Active warning. Find the fuse box under the dashboard or in the engine bay. Use the owner's manual to identify the correct fuse for accessory power. Pull the fuse out with a fuse puller or tweezers. Inspect it for a broken wire or dark spots. Replace it with a fuse of the same rating. Test the system by turning on the ignition.

### Ignition Switch Adjustment

The ignition switch might not register the key position correctly. Insert the key and turn it to the accessory position. Wiggle the key gently while observing the dashboard lights. If the light flickers or stays on, the switch may need adjustment. Locate the ignition switch behind the steering column. Tighten any loose screws or bolts. This can improve contact and fix the issue. Avoid forcing the key to prevent damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Causes Full Accessory Power Active In Ford Explorer?

Full Accessory Power Active usually occurs due to a faulty ignition switch or electrical issues. It can also be triggered by a malfunctioning Body Control Module (BCM). Identifying the root cause is essential for proper repair and to avoid battery drain or starting problems.

### How To Reset Full Accessory Power Active Warning?

To reset the warning, turn off the ignition and remove the key. Wait for a few minutes, then restart the vehicle. If the warning persists, disconnecting the battery for a short time can help reset the system, but ensure you have radio and security codes.

### Can A Dead Battery Cause Full Accessory Power Active?

Yes, a dead or weak battery can trigger this warning. Low voltage affects the vehicle’s electrical system, causing erroneous alerts. Replacing or recharging the battery often resolves the issue. Always test the battery health if the warning appears.

### When Should I See A Mechanic For This Warning?

If the warning persists after basic resets, consult a mechanic. Persistent issues might indicate deeper electrical or ignition system faults. Early professional diagnosis prevents further damage and ensures your Ford Explorer operates safely and reliably.
