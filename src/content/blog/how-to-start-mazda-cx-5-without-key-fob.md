### Checking And Replacing Battery

Start by examining your key fob for signs of a low battery, such as reduced range or inconsistent response. You can open the fob using a small screwdriver or a coin—look for a slot on the side to gently pry it apart.

Inside, you’ll find a small coin-cell battery, usually a CR2032 or similar. Check the battery’s voltage with a multimeter if you have one, or simply replace it if it looks old or corroded. Make sure to insert the new battery with the positive side up, then snap the fob back together securely.

Have you ever ignored a weak key fob battery only to be locked out? That experience taught me to carry a spare battery in my glove box. It’s a small step that can prevent a big headache.

### Temporary Solutions To Power Issues

If you find yourself with a dead key fob battery and no replacement on hand, there are a couple of quick fixes you can try. Some Mazda CX-5 models allow you to start the car by holding the key fob close to the start button or a designated spot inside the vehicle.

Check your owner’s manual for the exact location, as this trick bypasses the need for a strong wireless signal. You can also try tapping the key fob gently or pressing buttons firmly to improve contact temporarily.

Another tip is to remove the battery for a few seconds and put it back in, which might reset the fob’s internal chip. These temporary hacks won’t fix the battery issue permanently but can get you moving in a pinch.
