**If you’re working on your 350 Chevy engine, putting in the distributor might seem tricky at first. But don’t worry—once you know the steps, it’s a job you can handle yourself.**

Getting your distributor installed correctly is key to making sure your engine runs smoothly and starts every time. In this guide, you’ll learn exactly how to put a distributor in your 350 Chevy, with simple instructions that anyone can follow.

Keep reading, and by the end, you’ll feel confident tackling this important task on your own.



## Gather Required Tools

Before you dive into putting a distributor in your 350 Chevy, gathering the right tools is crucial. Having everything ready saves you from frustrating stops and starts. It also helps you work efficiently, cutting down the time you spend under the hood.

### Essential Tools List

* **Socket Set:**A good range of sockets, especially 1/2-inch drive, is necessary for bolts and fasteners.
* **Screwdrivers:**Both flathead and Phillips types will help with clamps and small screws.
* **Distributor Wrench:**This special wrench makes reaching the hold-down bolt easier in tight spaces.
* **Timing Light:**Essential for setting the ignition timing after installation.
* **Feeler Gauge:**Handy if you need to adjust points in older distributors.
* **Torque Wrench:**Ensures bolts are tightened to the correct specifications without overdoing it.
* **Dielectric Grease:**Keeps electrical connections safe from moisture and corrosion.

Missing even one of these can slow down your progress. I once started without a distributor wrench and struggled for hours trying to loosen a bolt in a tight spot. Trust me, grabbing the right tools first will save you headaches.

### Safety Equipment

Working on your engine means dealing with sharp parts, heavy components, and electrical connections. Protect yourself with safety glasses to shield your eyes from debris and gloves to avoid cuts and scrapes.

Also, consider wearing long sleeves to protect your arms from burns or scratches. A fire extinguisher nearby is wise, especially when working with ignition systems. Are you prepared to keep safety a priority during your installation?

## Prepare The Engine

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=preparing-the-engine-is-essential-before-installing-a-distributor-in-a-350-chevy-this-process-ensures-safety-and-makes-the-installation-smoother&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Preparing the engine is essential before installing a distributor in a 350 Chevy. This process ensures safety and makes the installation smoother. Taking careful steps helps avoid damage to engine parts.

Start by making sure the engine is cool. This prevents burns and other injuries. Have the right tools ready to work efficiently. Follow each step to keep the engine safe and ready for the new distributor.

### Disconnect Battery

Always disconnect the battery before working on the engine. This stops electrical shocks and shorts during the process. Use a wrench to loosen the negative battery cable first. Then remove the positive cable. Keep the cables away from the battery terminals.

### Remove Old Distributor

Locate the old distributor on the engine block. Note the position of the rotor and distributor cap. Remove the distributor cap by unscrewing or unclipping it. Label the spark plug wires to avoid confusion later.

Next, remove the hold-down bolt or clamp that secures the distributor. Carefully pull the distributor straight up. Do not force it, as this may damage the gears. Inspect the old distributor for wear or damage. Clean the mounting surface before installing the new one.

## Set Engine To Top Dead Center

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=setting-the-engine-to-top-dead-center-tdc-is-key-before-installing-the-distributor-on-a-350-chevy-tdc-means-the-piston-in-the-number-one-cylinder-is-at-its-highest-point&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Setting the engine to Top Dead Center (TDC) is key before installing the distributor on a 350 Chevy. TDC means the piston in the number one cylinder is at its highest point. This position ensures the ignition timing is accurate. Without setting TDC, the engine won’t run properly.

The process starts by identifying the number one cylinder. Then, rotate the crankshaft carefully until the piston reaches TDC. This step guarantees the distributor aligns with the engine’s firing order. Proper alignment prevents engine misfires and poor performance.

### Identify 1 Cylinder

The number one cylinder is at the front of the engine on the driver’s side. Look at the engine block to find the first spark plug wire. This wire connects to the number one cylinder. Mark it clearly to avoid confusion during the installation.

### Rotate Crankshaft

Use a socket wrench on the crankshaft pulley bolt. Turn the crankshaft clockwise slowly. Watch the timing marks on the harmonic balancer or pulley. Align the mark with the pointer on the timing cover. This shows the number one piston at TDC on the compression stroke.




## Install New Distributor

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=installing-a-new-distributor-in-your-350-chevy-can-boost-engine-performance-this-task-requires-precision-and-attention-to-detail&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Installing a new distributor in your 350 Chevy can boost engine performance. This task requires precision and attention to detail. Follow these steps carefully to ensure a smooth installation. Remember, each step is crucial for optimal engine function.

### Align Distributor Gear

Start by aligning the distributor gear with the engine. Ensure the rotor points to the number one cylinder. This alignment is key for proper timing. Misalignment can lead to poor performance.

### Insert Distributor

Gently insert the distributor into the engine. Avoid forcing it into place. Twist it slightly to engage the gear. Ensure it seats flush with the engine block. A proper fit is crucial for functionality.

### Secure Distributor

Once inserted, secure the distributor in place. Tighten the hold-down clamp. This prevents movement during engine operation. Verify the distributor is stable and does not wobble.

## Adjust Distributor Timing

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=adjusting-the-distributor-timing-in-a-350-chevy-is-key-for-smooth-engine-performance-proper-timing-helps-the-engine-fire-at-the-right-moment&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Adjusting the distributor timing in a 350 Chevy is key for smooth engine performance. Proper timing helps the engine fire at the right moment. This improves power and fuel efficiency. Incorrect timing causes rough idling and poor acceleration. Follow these steps to adjust the distributor timing correctly.

### Connect Timing Light

Attach the timing light to the number one spark plug wire. Connect the power clips to the car battery. Turn on the engine and let it idle. Aim the timing light at the timing marks on the harmonic balancer. The light will flash in sync with the spark timing.

### Fine-tune Timing

Loosen the distributor hold-down clamp slightly. Rotate the distributor slowly left or right. Watch the timing marks move under the timing light. Adjust until the mark lines up with the recommended timing degree. The correct timing is usually found in the service manual.

### Tighten Distributor Clamp

Hold the distributor in place once timing is set. Tighten the hold-down clamp securely. Double-check the timing with the light to ensure no movement occurred. Turn off the engine and disconnect the timing light. Your distributor timing is now properly adjusted.




## Reconnect Components

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=after-installing-the-distributor-in-your-350-chevy-reconnecting-the-components-is-crucial-this-step-ensures-your-engine-runs-smoothly-and-fires-correctly&w=424&h=324&c=7)

After installing the distributor in your 350 Chevy, reconnecting the components is crucial. This step ensures your engine runs smoothly and fires correctly. Proper reconnection avoids misfires and engine damage. Take your time to connect each part carefully and correctly.

### Attach Spark Plug Wires

Match each spark plug wire to the correct distributor cap terminal. The wires are often numbered or marked for easy identification. Connect wires firmly to avoid loose connections that cause poor engine performance. Route the wires neatly to prevent contact with hot or moving parts. Use wire separators or clips if needed to keep wires organized and safe.

### Reconnect Battery

Before reconnecting, check battery terminals for corrosion or dirt. Clean terminals with a wire brush if necessary. Attach the positive battery cable first, then the negative cable. Tighten the clamps securely to ensure a good electrical connection. Reconnecting the battery restores power and allows you to test the distributor installation. Always follow safety precautions to avoid sparks or accidents.

## Test Engine Performance

Testing your engine's performance after installing a distributor in a 350 Chevy is crucial to ensure everything runs smoothly. This phase helps you catch any issues early and fine-tune the setup for optimal power and efficiency. Let's dive into how you can confidently test and adjust your engine.

### Start Engine

Before firing up your engine, double-check all connections on the distributor—wires, bolts, and vacuum lines should be secure. Turn the key and listen carefully as the engine cranks. If it doesn’t start right away, don’t panic; it might need a little more time for the distributor to properly sync with the ignition system.

Once the engine starts, watch for any unusual noises or hesitation. A steady idle is a good sign that the distributor is installed correctly. If your engine stalls or struggles to run, it’s time to look closer at the timing or wiring.

### Check For Smooth Operation

With the engine running, observe how it responds at idle and while you gently rev the throttle. The engine should accelerate smoothly without sputtering or misfiring. If you notice rough idling or backfiring, the distributor timing might be off.

Use a timing light to check the ignition timing against your Chevy 350’s specifications. This tool helps pinpoint if the spark is firing at the right moment in the combustion cycle. Accurate timing means better fuel efficiency and power, so don’t skip this step.

### Make Final Adjustments

Adjust the distributor by loosening the hold-down clamp just enough to rotate it slightly. Rotate the distributor slowly while the engine is running and observe changes in engine response. The goal is to find the position where the engine runs the smoothest and delivers the best throttle response.

Once you find the sweet spot, tighten the clamp firmly and recheck the timing with your light. Don’t forget to double-check all connections one last time. How does your engine feel now? If it’s running crisp and responsive, your distributor installation is a success.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Align The Distributor In A 350 Chevy?

Align the distributor by matching the rotor to the number one cylinder’s position. Ensure the engine is at top dead center (TDC) on the compression stroke before installing.

### What Tools Are Needed To Install A Distributor?

You’ll need a timing light, wrench set, distributor wrench, and a socket set. These tools help you position and secure the distributor properly.

### How Do I Set The Ignition Timing After Installation?

Use a timing light to adjust the distributor while the engine runs. Rotate the distributor until the timing marks align with the manufacturer’s specifications.

### Can A 350 Chevy Run Without A Distributor?

No, the distributor is essential for directing spark to the cylinders. Without it, the engine will not fire or run.
