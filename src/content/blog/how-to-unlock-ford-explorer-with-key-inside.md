## Common Reasons For Locking Keys Inside

Locking your keys inside your Ford Explorer can happen to anyone, and often when you least expect it. Understanding the common reasons behind this mishap can help you avoid it in the future. It’s not just carelessness—sometimes, it’s a simple oversight or a moment of distraction that leads to the door locking behind you.

### Accidental Door Lock Activation

Modern vehicles, including the Ford Explorer, often have automatic locking systems. You might accidentally press the lock button on your key fob while still holding the keys inside. Or, if your doors lock automatically after a certain time, you could be caught off guard. Have you ever rushed out and heard the locks click behind you without your keys?

### Leaving Keys On The Seat Or Dashboard

People often place their keys on the seat or dashboard while unloading groceries or kids. It’s an easy spot to forget, especially when you’re distracted. Walking away without the keys in hand can mean the doors lock with your keys still inside.

### Using Child Safety Locks

Child safety locks are great for keeping kids safe but can create challenges if you’re not familiar with how they work. You might lock the doors but not realize the child lock is engaged, preventing you from opening the door from inside. This can lead to a frustrating moment when you realize you’re locked in or out.

### Remote Key Fob Malfunction

Sometimes, the key fob battery is low or the device malfunctions, causing the vehicle to lock unexpectedly. You might think the car is unlocked, but it’s actually secured with your keys inside. Have you checked your fob’s battery recently?

### Distracted Or Rushed Exiting

Hurrying to meet someone or trying to multitask can easily cause you to forget your keys. You might close the door behind you without thinking twice. It’s a common scenario that leads to keys being trapped inside the vehicle.

## Using A Wedge And Rod Method

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=using-a-wedge-and-rod-to-open-your-ford-explorer-when-the-key-is-locked-inside-is-a-practical-approach-you-can-try-yourself-this-method-requires-patience-and-a-steady-hand-but-it-avoids-damage-to-your-vehicle&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Using a wedge and rod to open your Ford Explorer when the key is locked inside is a practical approach you can try yourself. This method requires patience and a steady hand, but it avoids damage to your vehicle. With the right tools and careful technique, you can create enough space to reach the unlock button inside your door.

### Tools Needed

* **Plastic wedge or door stop:**To gently pry open the door without scratching the paint.
* **Long, thin rod or coat hanger:**To reach inside and press the unlock button.
* **Protective gloves:**To keep your hands safe and improve grip.
* **Towel or soft cloth:**To place around the wedge, preventing damage to the door frame.

Having these tools ready helps you work efficiently and safely. Using a plastic wedge is better than a metal one since it reduces the risk of damage. If you don’t have a plastic wedge, a wooden door stop covered with a cloth can work as a substitute.

### How To Create A Gap

Start by wrapping the plastic wedge with a towel to protect your car’s paint. Then, carefully insert the wedge between the top corner of the door and the frame. Apply gentle pressure to create a small gap—enough to slip the rod inside.

Be patient as you work the wedge in. If the door won’t budge, try adjusting the angle slightly. Have you noticed how a small change in pressure can make a big difference? Avoid forcing the wedge, or you risk bending the door frame.

### Reaching The Unlock Button

Once the gap is open, slide the rod inside slowly. Use a flashlight to help you see the lock mechanism clearly. Aim to press the unlock button or pull the manual lock up, depending on your Explorer’s model.

Keep your movements steady and gentle. If you miss the button the first time, reposition the rod and try again. This process might take a few attempts, but staying calm improves your chances of success.

## Using Fordpass App For Keyless Entry

The FordPass app offers a simple way to access your Ford Explorer without a physical key. This app lets you control your vehicle remotely using your smartphone. It is especially helpful when your keys are locked inside the car. The app connects directly with your car’s system for quick, secure entry.

### Setting Up Fordpass

Begin by downloading the FordPass app from the App Store or Google Play. Create an account using your email and a strong password. Add your Ford Explorer to the app by entering its VIN number. Follow the on-screen instructions to link your vehicle. Ensure your phone has a stable internet connection during setup. The app will verify your car and sync settings automatically.

### Unlocking Your Explorer Remotely

Open the FordPass app on your phone. Select your Ford Explorer from the list of connected vehicles. Tap the “Unlock” button to send a remote unlock command. Wait a few seconds for the car doors to unlock. The app will confirm the action once complete. Keep your phone nearby to maintain communication with your vehicle. This process works even if the keys are inside the car.
