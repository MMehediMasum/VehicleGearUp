## Junkyards And Salvage Yards

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=junkyards-and-salvage-yards-are-often-overlooked-but-valuable-places-to-find-used-car-batteries-at-a-fraction-of-the-cost-these-locations-collect-and-store-vehicles-that-are-no-longer-roadworthy-making-them-a-treasure-trove-for-parts-still-in-good-condition&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Junkyards and salvage yards are often overlooked but valuable places to find used car batteries at a fraction of the cost. These locations collect and store vehicles that are no longer roadworthy, making them a treasure trove for parts still in good condition. If you’re on a budget or need a quick replacement, exploring these yards can save you both money and time.

### What To Look For In Used Batteries

Not all used batteries in junkyards are worth buying. Look for batteries that are clean and free of corrosion around the terminals. Check the battery case for cracks or leaks, as these signs mean the battery is likely damaged beyond repair.

Ask the yard staff about the battery’s age and if it’s been tested recently. A battery less than three years old generally holds more charge and lasts longer. Also, check the battery’s label for cold cranking amps (CCA), which tells you how much power the battery can deliver in cold weather—a crucial factor for reliable starts.

### Safety Precautions And Testing

Handling car batteries requires care because they contain acid and can emit harmful gases. Always wear gloves and eye protection when inspecting or removing a battery. Avoid smoking or open flames near the battery to prevent accidents.

Before installing a used battery, test it with a multimeter or have the junkyard perform a load test. This testing ensures the battery holds a charge and can deliver enough power to start your vehicle. If you don’t have the tools, many auto parts stores offer free battery testing, which is worth the effort to avoid buying a dud.
