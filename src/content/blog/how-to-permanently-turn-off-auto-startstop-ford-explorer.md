## Auto Start/stop Feature Explained

The Auto Start/Stop feature in the Ford Explorer is designed to improve fuel efficiency and reduce emissions. It automatically shuts off the engine when the vehicle is idle and restarts it when you press the accelerator. Understanding how this system works and why it’s included can help you decide if you want to keep it enabled or turn it off permanently.

### Purpose And Benefits

The main goal of the Auto Start/Stop feature is to save fuel. When you’re stopped at a traffic light or in heavy traffic, the engine turns off, reducing unnecessary fuel consumption.

This not only lowers your fuel costs but also cuts down on harmful emissions. If you care about the environment or want to stretch every gallon of gas, this feature can be helpful.

Have you ever noticed how much time you spend idling during your daily commute? Even short stops add up, and Auto Start/Stop targets these moments to make a difference.

### How It Works In Ford Explorer

When you come to a complete stop and keep your foot on the brake, the system automatically shuts off the engine. As soon as you lift your foot from the brake or press the accelerator, the engine restarts instantly.

The system uses sensors to monitor conditions like battery charge, cabin temperature, and engine temperature. If any condition isn’t ideal, the Auto Start/Stop won’t activate, ensuring your comfort and safety.

You might feel a slight pause or hesitation when the engine restarts, which some drivers find distracting. Knowing how and when this happens can help you decide if turning off the feature is right for your driving style.

### Using The Vehicle Settings Menu

Accessing the vehicle settings menu is one of the most direct ways to manage the Auto Start/Stop feature. Start by pressing the “Settings” button on your dashboard or touchscreen display.

Navigate to the “Vehicle” or “Driver Assistance” section where you will find the Auto Start/Stop option. From here, you can toggle it off to prevent the engine from shutting off automatically when you come to a stop.

This method gives you control through the car’s own system, ensuring that the setting remains consistent every time you drive—no need to reset it with every ignition cycle.

### Physical Button Method

Your Ford Explorer often includes a physical button, usually labeled with an “A” surrounded by a circular arrow, which controls the Auto Start/Stop feature.

Pressing this button disables the feature for your current drive session. However, most models will reactivate Auto Start/Stop once you turn the vehicle off and on again.

Keep in mind, this is a quick fix rather than a permanent solution. If you want to avoid toggling this button each time, explore other options in the settings menu.

### Temporary Vs. Permanent Deactivation

Understanding the difference between temporary and permanent deactivation can save you frustration.

* **Temporary deactivation**is usually done via the physical button and will reset once you restart your vehicle.
* **Permanent deactivation**involves changing the settings through the vehicle menu, which stays in effect until you change it back.

Ask yourself: How often do you want Auto Start/Stop off? If it’s every time you drive, the menu settings are your best bet. If it’s only in certain situations, the physical button offers quick control without committing to a permanent change.

The auto start/stop system helps save fuel by shutting off the engine when your vehicle is idle, such as at stoplights. Modifying your vehicle’s auto start/stop system could potentially affect your warranty coverage. Ford’s warranty terms may not cover damage or issues resulting from disabling factory settings. It’s a good idea to check your warranty details or consult your dealer before making permanent changes.

Safety is another factor to consider. The auto start/stop is integrated with your Explorer’s overall engine management and emissions controls. Disabling it improperly might cause unexpected behavior or sensor errors.

Have you experienced any warning lights or engine issues after attempting to disable the feature? If not, that’s great, but proceed with caution and make sure any changes are done correctly to avoid costly repairs.
