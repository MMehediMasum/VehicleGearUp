The Ford Escape comes standard with two rows of seating. This setup comfortably seats five passengers—two in the front and three in the back. The rear seats often fold down, giving you extra cargo space when needed.

This layout works well if you usually travel with a small group or need extra room for gear. Have you ever found yourself wishing for more space during a road trip or grocery run? The standard layout balances passenger comfort with cargo flexibility.

### Availability Of Third Row

Many wonder if the Ford Escape includes a third row of seats for extra passengers. Currently, the Escape does not offer a third row option. If you need seating for seven or more, you might want to consider larger SUVs in Ford’s lineup, like the Explorer or the Expedition.

Choosing a vehicle with third-row seating means thinking about your regular passenger count and cargo needs. ## Impact On Performance And Fuel Economy

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=the-addition-of-a-third-row-in-the-ford-escape-isnt-just-about-extra-seats-it-directly-affects-how-the-vehicle-drives-and-how-much-fuel-it-consumes-these-changes-matter-if-you-want-a-smooth-ride-without-constantly-stopping-at-the-pump&w=424&h=324&c=7)

The addition of a third row in the Ford Escape isn’t just about extra seats; it directly affects how the vehicle drives and how much fuel it consumes. These changes matter if you want a smooth ride without constantly stopping at the pump. Understanding these impacts helps you decide if a third row fits your lifestyle and driving habits.

### Weight And Handling

Adding a third row increases the vehicle’s weight significantly. This extra weight can make the Escape feel less nimble and slower to respond in corners or quick maneuvers.

You might notice a difference if you often drive on winding roads or need to park in tight spots. The handling feels a bit heavier, and the steering might require more effort. This change doesn’t make the Escape unsafe, but it does shift its driving dynamics.

Have you ever felt your car struggle when fully loaded? That’s similar to what happens with the third row—it’s like carrying an extra passenger all the time, even when the seats are empty.

### Fuel Efficiency Considerations

More weight means the engine works harder, which directly affects fuel economy. Expect a drop in miles per gallon compared to the two-row version of the Escape.

This decrease varies depending on your driving style and road conditions. City driving with frequent stops can worsen fuel consumption, while steady highway speeds might lessen the impact.

If saving fuel is a priority, think about how often you’ll actually need the third row. Is the extra seating worth the extra trips to the gas station? Your answer could guide which model suits you best.
