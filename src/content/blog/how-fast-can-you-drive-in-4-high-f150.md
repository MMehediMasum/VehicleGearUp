## 4 High Mode Basics

The 4 High mode in a Ford F150 is designed for driving on slippery or rough roads. It provides extra traction by sending power to all four wheels. This mode keeps your speed steady while improving control and stability. Understanding how and when to use 4 High helps you drive safely in various conditions.

### When To Use 4 High

Use 4 High on roads with snow, ice, or loose gravel. It works well at moderate speeds up to 55 mph. Avoid using 4 High on dry pavement. It can cause tire wear and strain the drivetrain. Switch to 4 High when you need better traction but still want to maintain normal driving speed.

### How 4 High Affects Driving

4 High changes how your truck handles by powering all wheels. This improves grip on slippery surfaces and prevents slipping. The truck may feel heavier and less responsive in turns. Fuel efficiency may drop slightly because of extra power use. Drive smoothly and avoid sudden moves to keep control while in 4 High.

## Comparing 4 High With Other Modes

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=understanding-how-fast-you-can-drive-your-f150-in-4-high-requires-knowing-the-differences-between-driving-modes-each-mode-has-its-own-speed-limits-and-best-uses&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Understanding how fast you can drive your F150 in 4 High requires knowing the differences between driving modes. Each mode has its own speed limits and best uses. Comparing 4 High with other modes helps you choose the right setting for the road and conditions.

### 4 Low Vs 4 High Speed Limits

4 Low is designed for slow, tough terrain. It provides extra torque but limits top speed. Driving faster in 4 Low can damage the vehicle. Typically, 4 Low should not exceed 25 mph.

4 High allows higher speeds while still giving four-wheel drive power. It is best for slippery roads or light off-road use. You can drive up to about 55-65 mph in 4 High. This makes it suitable for snow, rain, or gravel roads.

### 2wd Mode Speed Considerations

2WD mode powers only two wheels and is best for dry, paved roads. It allows normal highway speeds without risking drivetrain damage. You can drive at the legal speed limit in 2WD safely.

Using 2WD on slippery surfaces reduces traction and control. Switching to 4 High improves grip at moderate speeds. Always choose the mode based on road conditions, not just speed limits.
