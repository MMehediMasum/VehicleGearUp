## Ford Escape Towing Basics

Understanding the basics of towing your Ford Escape is essential before you hit the road with a trailer or another vehicle in tow. Knowing whether your Escape can be flat towed, and how to do it properly, can save you time, money, and potential damage to your vehicle.

### What Is Flat Towing

Flat towing means pulling your vehicle behind another without any part of it lifting off the ground. In this setup, all four wheels of your Ford Escape stay on the road while it's being towed. This method is different from using a trailer or dolly, where wheels are lifted or off the ground.

Flat towing is popular among RV owners who want to bring their Escape along for local driving once they reach their destination. But can every Ford Escape be flat towed? That depends on the model year, transmission type, and whether you follow the manufacturer's guidelines exactly.

### Why Choose Flat Towing

Flat towing offers several advantages that make it appealing if you often tow your Escape behind another vehicle.

* **Convenience:**You don’t need a separate trailer or dolly, saving you space and setup time.
* **Cost-effective:**Avoid rental or purchase costs of towing equipment like trailers.
* **Easy access:**Your Escape remains ready to drive immediately when you stop, without unloading it from a trailer.
* **Less wear and tear:**Compared to hoisting your vehicle on a trailer, flat towing reduces stress on the drivetrain when done correctly.

Have you ever wondered if your Escape’s transmission could handle flat towing without damage? This is a key concern since improper towing can cause expensive repairs.

If you plan to flat tow your Escape, you must check your owner's manual or contact Ford directly. Not every Escape model supports flat towing, and some require specific steps, like placing the transmission in neutral or disconnecting the driveshaft.
