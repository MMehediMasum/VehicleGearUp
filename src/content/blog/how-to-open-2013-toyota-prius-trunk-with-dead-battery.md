**If you own a 2013 Toyota Prius, you know how handy the trunk can be for carrying everything you need. But what happens when your car battery dies and you can’t open the trunk the usual way?**

It can be frustrating and stressful, especially if you have important items inside. Don’t worry—this guide will show you exactly how to open your Prius trunk even when the battery is dead. By the end, you’ll have a simple, clear solution that saves you time and hassle.

Keep reading—you’re just a few easy steps away from unlocking your trunk without any extra tools or trips to the mechanic.




## Locate The Emergency Trunk Release

Locating the emergency trunk release in a 2013 Toyota Prius is essential when the battery is dead. This release allows access to the trunk without using power. It is a manual way to open the trunk in case of emergencies. Knowing its location saves time and avoids frustration.

### Find The Release Handle Inside The Trunk

The emergency trunk release handle is inside the trunk. It is usually a small, brightly colored lever or handle. You can find it near the latch mechanism on the trunk lid. The handle often glows in the dark for easy spotting. It is designed to be simple and easy to pull.

### Use The Release In Case Of Power Failure

If the car battery is dead, the electronic trunk opener will not work. Pull the emergency release handle firmly. This action manually unlocks the trunk latch. The trunk will then open without any power from the car. This method ensures you can access the trunk anytime, even during a power failure.

## Access The Trunk From The Rear Seats

Accessing the trunk of your 2013 Toyota Prius when the battery is dead might seem tricky at first. However, you can reach the trunk from inside the car by using the rear seats. This approach avoids the need for external tools or professional help, making it a practical choice.

### Fold Down The Rear Seatbacks

Start by locating the release levers for the rear seatbacks. These are usually found near the top of the seatbacks or on the side edges. Pull the levers to fold the seats forward, creating a clear path to the trunk area.

Folding the seats takes just a few seconds and opens up a spacious area to work through. If you have cargo or items in the back seat, move them out of the way first to make the process smoother.

### Reach Through To Unlock The Trunk

Once the rear seats are folded down, you can reach through the opening to find the trunk release latch. It’s located near the center of the trunk’s interior wall. Feel around carefully to locate the lever or handle.

Pulling this latch manually will release the trunk lock, allowing you to open it despite the dead battery. Have you ever tried reaching in without a clear view? It can be a bit of a challenge, but persistence pays off.

## Use The Mechanical Key

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=the-mechanical-key-fits-into-a-hidden-slot-on-the-drivers-door-turn-it-to-unlock-the-door-and-access-the-trunk-release-inside&w=424&h=324&c=7)

The mechanical key fits into a hidden slot on the driver’s door. Turn it to unlock the door and access the trunk release inside. This method works even if the battery is dead.

Unlocking the trunk of a 2013 Toyota Prius with a dead battery can seem challenging. But, the mechanical key offers a simple solution. This key provides access without relying on battery power. Follow these steps to open your trunk manually and gain entry with ease.

### Remove The Key From The Key Fob

The key fob contains a hidden mechanical key. Find the release button on the back of the fob. Press it and pull the key out gently. This small key is your tool for manual access.

### Locate The Trunk Lock Cylinder

Next, find the trunk lock cylinder. It's located near the rear badge or emblem. This is where the mechanical key will fit. Ensure you have a clear view for ease of access.

### Manually Unlock The Trunk

Insert the key into the lock cylinder. Turn it gently to the right. This action will manually unlock the trunk. Carefully lift the trunk lid to open it. Now, you can access the items you need inside.



## Jump-start The 12v Battery

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=jump-starting-the-12v-battery-in-your-2013-toyota-prius-is-a-practical-way-to-access-the-trunk-when-the-battery-is-dead-this-approach-helps-you-avoid-costly-locksmith-services-or-towing-fees&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Jump-starting the 12V battery in your 2013 Toyota Prius is a practical way to access the trunk when the battery is dead. This approach helps you avoid costly locksmith services or towing fees. Understanding the battery's location and following safety steps ensures a smooth and safe jump-start process.

### Identify The Battery Location

In the 2013 Toyota Prius, the 12V battery is not under the hood like in many cars. Instead, it’s located on the passenger side, behind the glove box inside the cabin. You’ll need to carefully remove the glove box to access the battery compartment.

Knowing this helps you avoid wasting time searching under the hood. Have a flashlight handy to spot the battery terminals clearly.

### Safety Precautions Before Jump-starting

Jump-starting a battery can be risky if you don’t take precautions. Always wear safety glasses and gloves to protect yourself from sparks or acid leaks. Make sure both vehicles (if using another car) are turned off and in park with the parking brakes engaged.

Avoid touching the metal clamps together. Also, don’t attempt a jump-start if the battery is visibly damaged or leaking. Have a fire extinguisher nearby just in case.

### Steps To Jump-start The Battery

* Connect the red (positive) jumper cable clamp to the positive terminal of the dead 12V battery.
* Attach the other red clamp to the positive terminal of the good battery in the booster vehicle or jump starter pack.
* Connect the black (negative) jumper cable clamp to the negative terminal of the good battery.
* Attach the other black clamp to an unpainted metal surface on the Prius away from the battery—this acts as a ground.
* Start the booster vehicle or turn on the jump starter and let it run for a few minutes.
* Try to start the Prius and check if the electrical systems power up.
* Once the Prius starts, carefully remove the jumper cables in the reverse order of connection.

Have you ever struggled with a dead battery in an unexpected moment? Preparing yourself with these clear steps can turn a stressful situation into a quick fix.

## Preventive Tips For Future Access

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=knowing-how-to-open-your-2013-toyota-prius-trunk-when-the-battery-is-dead-is-crucial-but-preventing-this-issue-altogether-is-even-better-taking-a-few-simple-steps-can-save-you-from-frustrating-lockouts-and-unexpected-delays&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Knowing how to open your 2013 Toyota Prius trunk when the battery is dead is crucial, but preventing this issue altogether is even better. Taking a few simple steps can save you from frustrating lockouts and unexpected delays. Here are some practical tips to help you maintain easy access to your trunk in the future.

### Regular Battery Maintenance

Keeping your Prius battery in good shape is the first step to avoid trunk access problems. Check the battery terminals for corrosion and clean them regularly. Make sure the battery charge stays healthy by driving your car often or using a trickle charger if it sits unused for long periods.

Have you ever experienced a dead battery right when you needed to grab something from your trunk? Taking a few minutes each month to inspect your battery can prevent this hassle and keep your car ready to go.

### Keep The Mechanical Key Accessible

Your Prius comes with a mechanical key hidden inside the key fob for emergencies. Keep this key somewhere easy to reach, like your wallet or a small pouch in your bag. It’s a simple backup that lets you open the trunk manually if the battery dies.

Many owners forget about this key until they really need it. Don’t let that be you. Knowing exactly where your mechanical key is can save a lot of stress.

### Install A Backup Power Source

Consider adding a small backup power source to your car’s electrical system. A portable jump starter or a dedicated auxiliary battery can provide enough power to unlock the trunk without needing a full jump start. This extra step is especially useful if you frequently use your Prius in cold weather or short trips where the main battery drains quickly.

Have you thought about how much time you spend waiting for roadside assistance? A backup power source puts control back in your hands and gets you moving faster.
